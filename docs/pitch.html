The problem

A vague sentence is cheap to write and expensive to act on

Unclear requirements can move decisions into review, testing, or production. ECS makes those decisions visible earlier.

"fixed retry issues"

Reviewers ask before they can review

The reviewer has to find out what changed and why before they can judge whether it is correct. That can add clarification rounds before review begins.

"so everything works smoothly"

Work comes back

Without observable results, acceptance depends on interpretation. Different expectations can lead to reopened work.

"make it more reliable"

Agents fill the gaps

An agent may infer a meaning for “reliable” that the team did not intend. Shared instructions and review help expose those assumptions.

What you get

A small kit for a shared habit

Name the actor. State the condition and outcome. Keep technical meaning exact. Show what is known and what still needs a decision.

Rules your agents can use

A portable skill with examples and a glossary guide, plus repository instructions and a Claude Code mirror. Verify loading in your client.

Templates your team can adapt

Six artifact templates, plus GitHub issue and PR templates and an Azure Repos PR template. Keep only the sections that help.

Tools for a careful rollout

An installer that skips conflicts, an optional writing checker, and a repeatable evaluation guide. Existing guidance stays available for manual merging.

ECS is an ASD-STE100-inspired team convention, not a certification. Instructions and clear prose do not replace technical review or tests.

A small first step

One repository. Two artifact types. Try two weeks.

Start with new user stories and PR descriptions. Leave the documentation backlog for later.

  1. Choose an owner and a baseline

    Agree on success criteria. Record clarification rounds, review time, and authoring effort for a comparable sample of recent work.

  2. Install, then try a real artifact

    Preview the installer on a pilot branch, review conflicts, and verify that your agent loads the skill. Review its output against your source facts.

  3. Decide from the results

    Compare similar work at the review date. Expand if the improvement justifies the writing effort. Adjust, extend a small sample, or stop if it does not.
